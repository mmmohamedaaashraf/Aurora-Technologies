Most rehabilitation happens where no one is watching.

A physiotherapist sees a patient for a short session, then sends them home with a sheet of exercises. The real work happens in the living room, without feedback.

Patients can't tell how far they actually raise the arm. They lean the trunk or hike the shoulder to make the movement look bigger without noticing, and the therapist only hears "I did them" at the next visit.

Axiora gives that home session a record: the range reached on each repetition, how many were done properly, and which compensations showed up.

What the camera measures

  • Joint angle against the trunk

    Shoulder elevation is measured relative to the patient's own trunk, following the International Society of Biomechanics shoulder convention. Leaning sideways does not inflate the number.

    Built

  • Repetitions, counted and graded

    Each repetition is marked full, partial, too fast or in the wrong plane. Jitter and half movements are not counted.

    Built

  • Compensation warnings

    Shoulder shrug, trunk side-bend, bent elbow and head tilt are flagged as they happen. Thresholds come from published research and the therapist can adjust them per patient.

    Built

  • Paced sessions from a prescription

    Raise, hold, lower and rest cues follow the tempo, sets and repetitions the therapist sets. Exercises come from a library built on published clinical guidance.

    Awaiting clinical review

Results so far

Tested on public research datasets and live sessions. We report the miss along with the wins.

Repetition counting

Repetitions counted correctly in all 20 sequences of the UI-PRMD public rehabilitation dataset.

0

False alarms on clean movement

In live testing, deliberate compensations were flagged on nearly every repetition, and clean repetitions raised no warnings.

8.4°

Abduction error against motion capture

Root-mean-square error for side-raise movements, compared with marker-based motion capture on the REHAB24-6 dataset, using subjects held out from development.

12.9°

Overall error, below our own target

Across all movements the error was 12.9° against a pre-registered target of 10°. It grows when the arm points toward the camera, where single-camera depth is weakest. That is the next engineering problem.

580+

Automated tests

Every change runs the full test suite and replays recorded sessions. A change that alters an earlier result is rejected.

Shihab: the exercise as a game

Home exercise is boring, and people stop doing it. Shihab shows the patient their own body as a glowing outline on screen, with a path for where the arm should go. A clean repetition shatters a crystal.

When the body compensates, the game doesn't take points away. The part of the body that is cheating lights up instead, which tells the patient what to fix. We chose this because research on rehabilitation games found that point penalties do little to reduce compensation.

How a session works

  1. The therapist prescribes

    Exercises, target range, tempo and sets, chosen from the library and adjusted to the patient.

  2. The patient sets up the camera

    A setup check makes sure the whole body is in frame and the camera angle is usable before the session starts.

  3. The patient exercises at home

    Live measurement, repetition grading and compensation warnings, inside the Shihab game or as a plain guided session.

  4. The therapist reviews progress

    Range reached, repetitions completed and compensations per session. This dashboard is the next thing we are building.
